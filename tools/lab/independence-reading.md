## Reading

- **No hidden redundancy.** In species 1-4 every precept but one has a witness, almost always
  Fux's own solution with one or two notes changed: each precept forbids something the others
  allow. The exception, `fs.final-octave-or-unison` in first species, follows from `fs.cadence`
  as the game encodes it: the first-species cadence rule demands the final unison or octave as well
  as the penultimate sixth or third. (In species 2-5 the cadence rule checks the approach only,
  and there the final rule is independent.) The redundancy is in the encoding, not in Fux, who
  states the two separately (p. 48; pp. 47, 49).
- **Fifth species.** `fis.quavers`, `fis.ligature` and `fis.florid` govern rhythm; the search varied
  pitches over Fux's own rhythms, so it could not break them. They are untested here, not shown
  redundant.
- **What the precepts alone allow.** The witnesses also show how much the precepts leave open: the
  third-species witness for the tritone leaps D4 to B5, a thirteenth, which no precept forbids as
  encoded (the lab's generators exclude leaps beyond the octave by their own local check, not by a
  rule). Whether Fux forbids leaps beyond the octave in so many words is to be checked. An earlier,
  blind search found first-species lines legal under every precept that repeat one note eight
  times: variety, too, Fux leaves to counsel, not precept.
