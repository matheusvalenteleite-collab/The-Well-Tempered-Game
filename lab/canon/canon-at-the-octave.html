Canon at the Octave

Write a two-bar subject in the treble. The bass imitates it an octave lower, two bars later, while the treble keeps going against it. You accept or refuse each new pair of bars. The last pair also has to sit under the subject, so the canon can repeat without end.

    The continuations follow two-voice florid counterpoint. Notes that start together, and every beat 1 and 3, must form consonances (3rds, 5ths, 6ths, 8ves, and their compounds; the 4th counts as a dissonance). Dissonances are allowed as passing or neighbour notes on weak parts of the beat, or as prepared suspensions that resolve down by step. Parallel 5ths and 8ves are not allowed, and the voices may not cross. Melodic leaps are limited to consonant intervals, and a large leap must be followed by a step back.

    On the first pass the bass is silent in bars 1–2. When the canon repeats, the bass plays bars 7–8 there, under the subject.
