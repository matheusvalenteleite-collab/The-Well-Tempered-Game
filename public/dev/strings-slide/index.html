Strings: the slide, without the volume boost

STRING CONSORT, Fugue I/4 (C sharp minor), first violin, bars 41 to 45. The same notes played seven ways by the game itself (the real engine, phone sound). You hear the player's violin alone, so the differences are easy to hear.

In each picture, the top half is pitch (grey blocks = the written notes, blue line = what sounds) and the bottom half is loudness (yellow line; the dashed grey line is the same notes joined thumb to thumb, for comparison). The small marks at the top are the slides. Tap a picture to jump there. Where a hand clip is shown (under the picture), it moves in step with the sound: watch how fast the thumb drags.

Loudness of each slid note, compared with the same note joined

dB, measured on the middle of each note through the phone-speaker model. Positive = louder than joined. Today's slide is up to 4.3 dB louder; the new versions are at or under the joined level.

What to decide

1. The volume fix: B (today's glide, the arriving note at its own level), or the new glissando (C and D), which also fixes it.

2. The glissando: does C (a quick drag) feel like a quick slide into the note, and D (a slow drag) like a gradual glide? The glide is always at most a quarter of the note, and lands on the written pitch.
