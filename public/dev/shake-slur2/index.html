Ornament slurs, part two: winds, voices, strings, and the sway

The same method as the first page (brass, GAME BOY, SWITCHED-ON, guitar): each instrument plays a real passage through the game's own engine, phone sound, your line alone. In each pair, A is the game today and B is the proposal. For the trills, the printed notes and their times are exactly the same in A and B; only how the instrument speaks them changes: one sound that moves from note to note, instead of a new attack on each. The last two pairs are about the thumb's sideways sway on a held note, not about ornaments.

In each trill picture, the top half is pitch (grey blocks = the printed notes, blue line = what you hear) and the bottom half is loudness (yellow; in B the dashed grey line is A). Tap a picture to jump there. Nothing here is in the game: it is all behind a switch that is off.

What was skipped

Nothing was skipped. One caution on the sampled instruments (oboe, flute, bassoon, the sung voices, the violin): the slur moves the pitch of the recording itself, a tone at most in these trills, so its colour moves a very little with it, as a real singer's or player's would not. The brass on page one does the same; whether these sound right is for you to hear.

What to decide

For each pair: is B what that instrument should do? Yes, no, or change it. The six trills are independent of each other, and so are the two sways.

A note on loudness: in the trills, A's re-struck notes sit a little under the held note (each starts from silence); in B the trill sits at the held note's level. The score never sets any of this. In the two sways, B has no swell at all: the note stays at the level it landed at; today's A swells by up to 4 dB on brass and up to 3 dB on the organ as you sway.
