# Fugue's data/touch/aof-entries.txt, D707). No published analysis of the 30 is in the repo; the statements are read
# from the notes by the Art of Fugue's scanner (tools/touch/aof-entries/scan.py; tools/touch/inv-entries/table.py
# drafted this table from its finds) and reviewed piece by piece.
# WHAT AN ENTRY IS HERE. The Inventions are not fugues: an entry is a statement of the Invention's subject (in
# Inventions 1, 7 and 13 the short motive, a half bar to a bar; in Inventions 2 and 8 the canon's subject, 2 a
# strict canon: dux and comes, each restatement of the head marked again). Where an Invention opens on two lines at
# once in double counterpoint, the second line is its countersubject and counted too (theme 2: Inventions 5, 6, 9, 11,
# 12, 14), as it changes hands. A Sinfonia's entries are its subject's, and its countersubject's where one returns
# with it (theme 2: Sinfonia 9's chromatic bass, Sinfonia 13's line against the answer). The scan found no fixed
# countersubject in Sinfonias 1-8, 10-12, 14, 15 (the line against the second entry restated fewer than twice at
# 0.8 over ten notes): there the subject alone.
#   S  scan.py: the theme's letter steps and rhythm laid from every note of every voice (prime and inverted; at its
#      values, and doubled for Inventions 1 and 7); kept where its head fits (one step off by one at most: a tonal
#      answer) and at least 0.75 of its notes (0.8 for Inventions 1, 2, 4, 7, 13; 0.7 for Sinfonia 11; fit printed),
#      or the same from its second note (a first note changed: "mod"). Other "mod" lines are D340's finders'
#      statements the scan's threshold misses (a head bent, a first note moved), checked by eye.
# Grammar as data/touch/aof-entries.txt (expert.ts): BAR+Q VOICE [flags]; U/L the two hands, S/A/B the three
# voices; len= the theme's length in quarter notes where it is read; t2 the countersubject; dux/comes a canon's parts.
