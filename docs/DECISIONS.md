| D62 | Fourth species, "a ligature wherever possible" (p. 74). Aloysius gives two licences for plain minims: necessity ("no room for a ligature") and variety (Josephus leaves out a possible ligature so as not to repeat the ligatures of the third and fourth bars; Aloysius approves on grounds of singing and elegance. Which bars he means depends on how the bar with the rest is counted; unresolved). Necessity is computed: a ligature was possible into bar b when some note, a singable interval from the downbeat of bar b-1, is consonant with that bar's cantus and, held over the bar line, is consonant with the next or a permitted dissonance that can resolve down by step. Every ligature in Fux's six solutions passes this test, and both his omissions (the downbeats of the sixth bar of Fig. 73 and of the fourth of Fig. 75, counting the bar with the rest as the first) were possible ligatures, i.e. both are variety. Variety is not reduced to a rule: Fux ends Fig. 73 with three 7-6s in a row and Fig. 74 with three 2-3s, so "no three alike" would condemn him; he also breaks chains with consonant ligatures rather than omissions. The count is taken from Fux (owner): each exercise allows as many voluntary omissions as his solution makes (1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0 for Figs. 73-78); the tasks without a solution allow one, the most he takes.
