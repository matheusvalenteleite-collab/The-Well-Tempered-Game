# The real play screen in the tutorial (design studio brief for the main board)

Approved by the owner on 31 October 2026 ("yes" to the main board building it). Mockup and tour:
the studio's page "Thumbs, round three", section 2.

## The problem

The tutorial plays on the play screen's box, but its strips are bare. A real round also shows the score,
the bar counter, ACCURACY and SUBJECTS, the crowd meter, the verdicts (NICE, MISS), the hint card, the legend
of the reading's marks and the stop buttons; the box adds the SUBJECT flag and effect and the other voices.
A player leaving the tutorial meets all of it at once.

## The rule: dress the stage gradually

Each element of the real screen comes on in the lesson that first needs it. It is introduced by one of Bach's
bubbles while the rest of the screen is dimmed, as lesson 1.1 does now. It stays on for every lesson after that.
The tutorial never shows a simplified screen again after THE REAL THING.

| When (D373/D379 numbering) | What comes on | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · First notes | the box, the gold line, the two strips, your voice's label | nothing else is needed for eight notes |
| 1.5 Playing with Bach | the other voices' inks, the voice bar (YOU · LOWER) | the first time another voice plays |
| 2 · High and low | the score, the verdicts, the bar counter | the first lessons that count notes |
| 3 · How long | the marks over the notes and their legend on the right strip | where the signs are taught |
| 3.6, or the start of Part 4 | ACCURACY, the crowd meter, the hint card | when a passage is judged as a whole |
| THE REAL THING (a new last lesson before the fugue track) | the SUBJECT flag and effect, SUBJECTS 0/1, the stop buttons, pause: the full screen | a fugue subject on a real instrument |

The main board's Parts 4–6 may move these points. The principle is what matters: one element per need, introduced once.

## THE REAL THING: its opening tour (Bach's words, one element lit at a time, the round paused)

1. The whole screen: "This is the real screen, the one you will play on. You know the box already; now the rest."
2. Score: "Your score. Every note you play well adds to it."
3. Bar counter: "Where you are: bar 2 of 31."
4. ACCURACY and SUBJECTS: "How exactly you played, and how many of the subject's entries you played whole."
5. Crowd: "The crowd. It grows when you play well and thins when you stumble."
6. Verdict: "My verdict on the note you just played."
7. Hint card: "My notes to you while you play. Read them in the rests." (Its placement is being redesigned
   in the studio: it covers the left thumb's playing area.)
8. SUBJECT flag: "Here the subject enters. Your voice has it; it glows while it lasts."
9. Legend: "The signs over the notes, explained beside them."
10. Stops: "The stops. One touch changes the sound, even while you play."
11. Voice bar and pause: "Your voice and the others. And pause, whenever you need it."

Then the lesson plays a fugue subject (Fugue 2's, on the organ, is the studio's suggestion) with the full screen.
