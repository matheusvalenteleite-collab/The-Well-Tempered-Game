# Ledbetter on the Book I preludes (and fugues): notes for gameplay

Read from the page-cited digest of David Ledbetter, *Bach's Well-tempered Clavier: the 48 preludes and
fugues* (Yale, 2002), Part Two (branch claude/vibrant-gates-fe6o3h, `docs/wtc/ledbetter/book1-*.md`),
24 October 2026. Printed pages. "n/d": not in the digest (a lossy conversion: ornament signs and time
signatures were lost; it records one prelude tempo change with bars, no. 2; no. 10's Presto without
bars; no fermatas). Each prelude: genre · texture · structure · ornaments · performance · the climax
challenge its own features suggest (to be checked against the score before it is built).

## Preludes

1. **C** — toccata arpeggiata crossed with the beginners' chord-shape prelude, "an elaboration of the most
   elementary form of improvisation" (pp. 143–4) · one broken-chord pattern, notated dry, meant wet (p. 144)
   · sequences bb. 8–11, 16–19; major-7th dissonances bb. 8, 16; chromatic bb. 22–3 dramatise the
   dominant pedal (score bb. 24–31) (pp. 143–7) · n/d · subtle rubato on dissonances (p. 143) ·
   **pedal point: hold G from b. 24.**
2. **c** — finger-exercise étude on a harmonic pattern (pp. 151–2) · moto perpetuo, cadenza style from
   b. 25 · bass to E♭ at b. 14; dominant pedal from b. 21; concert ending b. 26 to the end with the
   Presto/Adagio · n/d · Adagio = recitative freedom, no "stiff, pedantic exactitude" (bb. 33–4) (p. 152)
   · **pedal from b. 21, then the tempo changes (Presto, Adagio).**
3. **C♯** — expanding broken intervals, "brilliant Solfeggio"; a Rameau-like batterie in the added section
   (pp. 156–7) · permutations of an 8-bar phrase, each closed by a hemiola · bb. 63–98 "a long extension
   of the final dominant pedal"; staccato dashes on the last cadential chords (P 290) (pp. 157–8) · n/d ·
   lively · **the long dominant pedal; staccato chord stabs to close.**
4. **c♯** — siciliano, cantabile (p. 159) · siciliano rhythm, then quavers, in each bar · sonata shape:
   dominant b. 14; build-up b. 19–35 (Neapolitan b. 24, top b″ b. 25); climax: the bass's siciliano
   down to its lowest note bb. 30–1 (pp. 159–60) · arpeggio signs bb. 3, 8, 34 (pp. 160–1) · spread chords
   "rhythmically and expressively" (p. 160) · **arpeggiated chords; the bass's descent bb. 30–1.**
5. **D** — right-hand exercise on the thumb's pivot (p. 168) · n/d · subdominant reprise bb. 19–26; pedal
   from b. 27; cadenza-like close bb. 30–4 (p. 168) · arpeggio signs bb. 33–4 · n/d · **pedal from b. 27,
   arpeggiated cadenza chords.**
6. **d** — Corellian violin moto perpetuo (p. 170) · legatissimo, the inner parts telling (pp. 170–1) ·
   12-bar addition from b. 15: tonic pedal then dominant pedal; b. 23 into cadenza-style close (p. 171) ·
   n/d · **two pedals in a row (tonic, dominant) from b. 15, then the cadenza.**
7. **E♭** — sectional organ toccata with a fully developed fugue, "unique" (p. 174) · handshape figure;
   from b. 10 stile antico, tied dissonance; a double fugue (pp. 175–6) · "cathartic bass entry" b. 61
   (p. 176) · n/d · no change of tempo at b. 10 (p. 175) · **a texture per section; the climax at b. 61.**
8. **e♭** — tombeau, "Vivaldian slow movement" (pp. 178, 181) · florid line over repeated chords · top c‴
   b. 28, descent to bottom D♯ bb. 32–5, cadenza after b. 35 (pp. 178–81) · the densest ornament discussion
   of Book I: turn b. 13; written-out ornament b. 29; mordent on the first melody note in early copies;
   arpeggio direction (pp. 179–81) · slight inégalité, no overdotting (p. 178) · **ornament gestures (turn,
   mordent); the sweep bb. 28–35 into a free cadenza.**
9. **E** — pastorale (p. 183) · arpeggio as cantabile · aria-ritornello heard three times, "a pedal in place
   of a sequence"; dominant b. 8½, subdominant b. 15, literal reprise (p. 183) · n/d · n/d · **a gentle
   pedal hold in each ritornello (bars from the score).**
10. **e** — Clavier-Büchlein prelude with an added florid melody and a Presto second half; Corellian
    ornamentation (pp. 184–5) · melody over chords · Presto from A minor back to the tonic (bars n/d) ·
    trills fading into rests bb. 10, 12; d″ held while e″ restruck b. 12 (p. 186) · **the Presto.**
11. **F** — the arpeggio in an Allegro and a study of the "Doppelt-cadence" trill (Explication table) (p. 189)
    · 12/8 (24/16) · long trills bb. 12–15, the long left-hand trill bb. 14–15 (p. 189) · the trill study of
    the book · sustain the trills through the other hand · **keep the trill going while the other hand
    plays.**
12. **f** — cantabile moto perpetuo, "harpsichord sonority through overholding" (p. 191) · overheld broken
    chords · top c‴ b. 11; dominant cadence b. 12; interrupted cadence b. 16; dominant pedal rising to b. 20;
    release on the bottom C b. 21 (pp. 191–2) · n/d · "Nobody… could bear not to sustain these chords"
    (p. 192) · **the pedal's ramp to b. 20, the release at b. 21.**
13. **F♯** — "lighter French character" (p. 194) · échappée motif over 7–6 suspensions · binary at b. 15½;
    head entries in pairs in the second half (pp. 194–6) · a trill in associated sources · 12/16, not too
    lively · **call and answer in the second half.**
14. **f♯** — Couperin's progrès de quartes; a two-part invention; "wistful melancholy" (pp. 197–8) · two
    parts, three after the midpoint; chords bb. 14–15 · dominant b. 12½; the tonic return at b. 22,
    intensified (p. 197) · trills bb. 12, 18 (p. 203) · **trills at bb. 12 and 18; the return at b. 22.**
15. **G** — "the last of the finger-exercise preludes" (p. 200) · semiquavers in both hands from b. 13 ·
    bb. 14–17½ "build a more effective climax", top range (pp. 200–1) · n/d · light metre · **a burst of
    speed and range, bb. 13–18.**
16. **g** — a sonata Adagio; messa di voce as a trill (pp. 202–3) · bb. 1–11 develop b. 1, bb. 12–19 b. 2 ·
    n/d · the long trill: a ribattuta, from the main note, slowly, accelerating; not "an alarm clock"
    (Quantz, C. P. E. Bach) (pp. 202–3) · Adagio · **the accelerating long trill.**
17. **A♭** — aria-ritornello sonata in violin style (pp. 206–7) · bariolage bb. 13–16 · head in E♭ bb. 18–21;
    scales bb. 22–6; return from b. 35 (p. 207) · n/d · **the bariolage; the return at b. 35.**
18. **g♯** — the invention principle over a harmonic formula (p. 210) · circle of fifths bb. 3–4 · the
    episode from b. 19 intensifies: a chord a beat, two a beat, a hemiola; climax on a diminished 7th,
    b. 25 (p. 210) · slurred échappées b. 7 · the b. 24 chords like trumpet Bebung, legatissimo · **the ramp
    from b. 19 to b. 25.**
19. **A** — triple counterpoint laid out; a major-key lamento bass (p. 213) · lines graded; the running part
    migrating between the hands bb. 9–10 · episodes bb. 6–8, 11, 14–17; the close opens to the widest range
    (pp. 213–14) · inverted Anschlag b. 1 (p. 214) · characterise the three lines · **the running part
    passing between the hands; the widening close.**
20. **a** — an évolution of mordents for thumb and fifth finger; light, playful (p. 218) · n/d · rising bass
    from b. 25; a final mordent figure into the fugue (p. 218) · the mordent is the material · **mordents,
    peaking with the rising bass from b. 25.**
21. **B♭** — Fischer's sectional toccata-prelude (p. 220) · motoric bb. 1–10, freely rhetorical from b. 11
    (p. 221) · "adagio" at b. 11 in one copy: a manner, not a tempo; runs to the bottom (b. 12) and the top
    (b. 14) (p. 221) · n/d · **free rhetoric, bb. 11–14: runs across the whole keyboard.**
22. **b♭** — toccata/tastada arpeggiata of rhythmicised chords; lamento (p. 222) · the decoration moves into
    the bass from b. 3 · bass descent bb. 16–19 (p. 223) · n/d · dynamics from the number of notes in the
    chords; legatissimo (p. 223) · **fuller chords louder, over the descent bb. 16–19.**
23. **B** — "the middle three fingers only", a cantabile clavichord exercise (p. 226) · a rising scale pausing
    on the leading note · dominant bb. 3–6; relative minor b. 10; diminished 7ths from b. 8; four parts and
    the low F♯ kept for the last four bars (pp. 226–7) · n/d · **the four-part close onto the low F♯.**
24. **b** — Corelli trio-sonata prelude; a lamento of "yearning" (pp. 228–30) · diminution and close
    imitation after the double bar; syncopated alto from b. 42 (p. 229) · binary; a chromatic inflexion
    before the close (p. 229) · n/d · **the syncopations from b. 42 into the chromatic close.**

## Fugues (character, for sounds and voices)

1. Verset tradition, episodeless stretto fugue, "a study of line"; pedals at the end (pp. 147–9).
2. Venetian-concerto rhythm, "gnomic"; witty; rhetorical pause b. 28½ (pp. 153–4).
3. A bourrée; "one of Bach's sunniest"; concerto da capo (p. 158).
4. Five voices, three subjects; stile antico; grave, solemn (pp. 161–4).
5. French overture: dots, tiratas; grand, dramatic (p. 169).
6. Binary dance-sonata with inversion; minor-6th "sadness"; affective (pp. 171–4).
7. Light Vivaldi-ritornello sonata; a "jokey" C minor entry (pp. 176–7).
8. Learned modal ricercar; stretto, inversion, augmentation; ornaments expected (pp. 181–3).
9. "One of Bach's lightest and wittiest"; verset type (p. 183).
10. The only two-part fugue; octave unisons, Vivaldian rage aria (pp. 186–7).
11. A passepied; moto perpetuo (p. 190).
12. Chromatic; "as the pleurants accompany a bier"; a trill on the minim (pp. 193–4).
13. Standard shapes; "joyful progress" (pp. 196–7).
14. Stile antico (6/4) with sigh affects; deeply expressive; pauses (p. 198).
15. Gigue metre; closest to a Vivaldi concerto allegro (p. 201).
16. Fischer-model verset; "cantable Art"; stretto climaxes (pp. 204–6).
17. Vivaldian ritornello Spielfuge, tutti and solo (pp. 208–9).
18. Kerll verset, Hypodorian (pp. 210–12).
19. Partimento, learned, two subjects; a syncopated "Chinese puzzle" (pp. 214–16).
20. Anapaest drive; "grim doggedness", Vivaldian energy; a pedal for the final A (pp. 218–20).
21. Galant character piece; da capo; moderate (pp. 221–2).
22. Five voices, motet-style entries from the top down; dense stile antico (pp. 223–6).
23. A scale-segment argument; regular (pp. 227–8).
24. A 12-note "labyrinth"; despair; Largo but "somewhat quicker" than Adagio (pp. 231–4).
