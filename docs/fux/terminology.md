# Terms of art

**Species (*species contrapuncti*).** Five kinds of counterpoint against a cantus firmus in
semibreves: 1 note against note; 2 two minims; 3 four crotchets; 4 ligatures (syncopation);
5 florid (all combined).

**Cantus firmus.** The given melody in semibreves (Fux: "we invent it ourselves or take it from
a book of chorales", Mann 27). The six cantus firmi of first species serve throughout.

**Thesis / arsis.** Fux's terms for the downbeat and upbeat of the hand: *depressio manus* =
thesis, *elevatio* = arsis (1725 p. 56). Note that later writers often use them the other way
round (after the voice rather than the hand), as Mann remarks.

**Diminution (*diminutio*).** Filling the space of a skip with a passing note; the source of the
legitimate passing dissonance in second and third species (pp. 56, 64) and of the hidden-fifths
argument (pp. 49-50: improvising singers would fill the skip and expose the fifths).

**Nota cambiata.** "Exchanged note" (p. 64): in third species, a dissonant second crotchet left
by a skip of a third to a consonance. Fux's is the first written description of it (Mann 51 n.).

**Ligature, syncope (*ligatura*, *syncope*).** Fourth species: a minim in arsis tied to the
next bar's thesis. Consonant ligature: both halves consonant. Dissonant ligature: the tied
note becomes dissonant on the thesis and must resolve down by step (pp. 69-70).

**Battuta.** An octave (*ottava battuta*) reached on the downbeat from a tenth by stepwise
contrary motion; Fux leaves it to discretion (pp. 53-54).

***Mi contra fa*.** The tritone relation between the *mi* of one hexachord and the *fa* of
another (B against F): "mi contra fa est diabolus in musica" (p. 52). Forbidden as a melodic
leap (p. 52), the reason for the E-mode cadential sixth in second species (pp. 60-61) and for
accidental flats in third species (pp. 68-69).

**Hexachords.** Hard (G-E, with B = *mi*), natural (C-A), soft (F-D, with B♭ = *fa*); syllables
*ut re mi fa sol la*. Fux names tones by them (e.g. D = *la sol re*, p. 49).

**Modes (*toni*).** Six, by final: D, E, F, G, A, C, in this "natural order"; B is excluded
because its fifth is diminished (p. 50). Transposed modes are mentioned and deferred. The
counterpoint must be in the mode of the cantus (p. 48: the opening fifth below a D cantus
gives G).

**Perfect and imperfect consonances.** Perfect: unison, fifth, octave (and compounds).
Imperfect: thirds and sixths. The fourth is a dissonance in two voices. Imperfect consonances
are "more harmonious" and must predominate in first species; perfection belongs to beginning
and end (p. 46).

**Clefs.** Fux writes in C clefs (soprano, alto, tenor) and the F clef, using neighbouring clefs
for the two voices (p. 52). The game shows G and F clefs by register (D30); the original clefs
are kept in the dataset.

**Bicinium, tricinium.** Composition in two and in three voices.

**The harmonic triad (*trias harmonica*).** Third and fifth over the bass (1725 p. 82); in three
voices it should be complete in every bar unless a better melody or the avoidance of
consecutive perfect consonances calls for a sixth or octave instead.
