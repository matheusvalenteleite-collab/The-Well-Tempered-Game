# After a leap: principle, or regression to the mean?

Generated by `node tools/lab/leap-study.ts`. Von Hippel and Huron ("Why do skips precede reversals?
The effect of tessitura on melodic structure", *Music Perception* 18/1, 2000) argue that the turn
after a leap is largely regression to the mean: a leap tends to land far from the middle of the
line's range, and from there most moves go back. If so, a leap that does *not* land beyond the
middle should turn back no more often than a step that lands alike. Every move of Fux's lines
(repeated notes left out) is classed by size and by where it lands: **beyond** the line's median
pitch in the direction it moved, or **short** of it (at the median or on the side it came from).
The cells give the share of moves followed by a turn back, with counts and a 95% interval.

## The model: distance from the middle, leap size, species

Odds ratios for a turn back (95% bootstrap interval). Distance: per six semitones landed beyond the line's median in the direction of the move. Third and leap: against a step landing at the same distance, in the same species.

| corpus | distance (per 6 semitones) | a third | a fourth or more |
|---|---|---|---|
| two voices, species 1-5 (species held apart) | 5.24 (3.87–7.88) | 3.29 (1.92–5.85) | 8.48 (5.25–15.59) |
| two voices, first species only | 2.48 (1.29–7.13) | 1.05 (0.39–4.97) | 2.01 (0.53–28.41) |
| species 2-5, only moves landing on a consonance (species held apart) | 6.78 (4.63–11.53) | 2.34 (1.30–4.08) | 6.05 (3.68–11.86) |
| three voices, added voices | 6.83 (3.55–18.15) | 0.71 (0.34–1.31) | 0.79 (0.30–2.42) |
| cantus firmi | 10.60 (5.05–42.35) | 3.60 (2.39–6.36) | 4.52 (2.76–32.64) |

**Reading.** Where a line lands relative to its middle matters everywhere, and strongly: each six
semitones beyond the median multiply the odds of turning back by about five to ten. What leap
size adds once that is held fixed depends on the texture:

- In whole-note lines (first species; the added voices in three voices) it adds nothing
  measurable: in three voices the odds ratio for a leap of a fourth or more is 0.8 (interval
  0.3-2.4); in first species, with few leaps, the interval is too wide to say (0.5-28). There the
  turn after a leap is what von Hippel and Huron say it is: regression to the mean.
- In the running lines of species 2-5 a leap turns back far more often than a step landing at
  the same distance (odds about 6-8), and this survives keeping only moves that land on a
  consonance (so it is not the rule that a dissonant passing note goes on by step). But the
  comparison is with steps, and steps in running lines go on in the same direction (scales): the
  contrast may be the inertia of steps as much as a rule for leaps. These data cannot separate
  the two.
- Fux's cantus firmi show a leap effect too (odds 4-5, wide interval), but they are few and were
  composed to a convention that may include the turn itself (D8, constraint 11).

So the habit reported in the habits study ("after a leap of a fourth or more he turns back 76% up,
95% down") is, in whole-note writing, a shadow of the range rather than a principle; in running
lines it is real but not yet explained.
