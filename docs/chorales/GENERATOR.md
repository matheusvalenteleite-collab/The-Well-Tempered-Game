# The chorale generator: steps 1-2 (cadence plan, chords)

Generated by `node tools/chorales/generator_report.ts`. Code: `src/chorale/generate/`; training data:
`data/chorales/generator/bach-events.json` (`tools/chorales/generator_data.py`). Chords are Kirnberger's
fundamentals as degrees of the chorale's key, one under every on-beat melody note, as at level 2.
Every number is measured on melodies the models have not seen.

## 1. Bach's chorales, every setting of the tune held out

370 chorales, 235 tunes, 2277 phrase ends, 16511 melody notes.

| what the generator is given | its most likely choice is Bach's |
|---|---|
| the melody: the cadence chord at each phrase end (step 1) | 74% of phrase ends |
| the melody: the chords, with no plan | 49% of notes |
| the melody and its own best plan (steps 1 and 2) | 53% of notes |
| the melody and Bach's own cadence plan (step 2 alone) | 57% of notes |
| *benchmark:* two of Bach's settings of one tune (75 pairs with the same melody degrees) | 75% of notes |

## 2. Kittel's 24 melodies: eight settings each, beside his eight basses

For each melody the generator makes as many settings as Kittel has basses (8, or 9), trained on Bach's
chorales without Bach's settings of that tune; Kittel's basses are never trained on. A chord is *attested*
when Kittel or Bach puts the same chord under that note. Kittel's own basses are measured the same way,
each against his other basses and Bach (the benchmark: what a master's set of variants looks like).

| | chords attested | at phrase ends | Bach's chord (where Bach set the tune) | Kittel's chords found | different chords per note | different cadence plans |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kittel's basses** (each against the others) | 91% | 95% | 48% | 100% | 2.5 | 4.6 |
| **generator**, diverse settings | 82% | 90% | 45% | 72% | 2.5 | 4.7 |
| generator, its single most likely setting | 90% | 98% | 54% | 43% | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| baseline: each chord drawn alone from Bach's habit at the melody degree | 71% | 64% | 40% | 61% | 2.4 | 5.6 |

Columns: *Kittel's chords found*: the share of the distinct chords Kittel uses under a note that one of the
generated settings also uses. *Different chords per note*: across the eight. *Different cadence plans*:
among the eight, per melody.

**Variety against attestation.** The more the settings differ, the more of their chords neither master uses.
The generator's two settings (temperature T of the draws, the share of the likelier draws kept) trace the trade-off;
the default (T = 0.8, half kept) is the point with Kittel's variety. These two numbers, and the weight of a
different cadence when the eight are chosen (3 chords: it gives Kittel's 4.6 cadence plans per melody, where 1 gives
3.7, at no cost in attestation), are the only ones chosen by looking at Kittel's basses.

## Reading

- **Step 1 works as the level-1 study said it would**: the most likely cadence chord is Bach's at 74% of phrase
  ends, and at Kittel's phrase ends the generated cadences are attested 93% of the time (Kittel's own 95%).
- **Step 2 is about where Kittel stands against Bach.** The single most likely setting has Bach's chord under
  54% of the notes of Kittel's melodies; Kittel's basses have it under 48% on average. Against Bach's own
  chorales the figure is 53% (57% with Bach's cadence plan given). The benchmark of 75% (two of Bach's settings of
  one tune) is high because the pairs with identical melodies are often near-copies; across the Kittel-Bach
  concordance two of Bach's settings share the root under 69% of notes (docs/chorales/KITTEL-BACH.md).
- **Eight settings are where the gap is.** With Kittel's variety, 82% of the generated chords are attested against
  91% of his. The chain knows one chord of context and the home key only: inside a modulation it does not know
  the local key, and it cannot plan a phrase as a whole. Unattested is not wrong (the 'baseline' row shows what
  wrong-ish looks like: 64% at cadences), but it is where to look first. Next refinements, each to be measured
  here: the local key of each phrase (from its cadence), two chords of context, and the harmonic rhythm.
- Not yet: the bass (inversions, step 3), the figures, the inner voices (step 4). A setting is chords only.
