## Reading

- **Bach's habits are what make the voices his.** With them, the generated alto and tenor are Bach's own notes
  58% and 55% of the time, and sound his two notes 68% of the time; without them (the
  smoothest voice-leading alone) about a third. Under Kittel's basses they sound the notes of Bach's own settings of the
  tune 54% of the time (habits off: 26%).
- **The judge has little to say about them**: under 1 parallel fifth or octave per 100 chords, no augmented second,
  the figure's chord complete; what remains is mostly a left-out fifth (which Bach also does) and the occasional rare
  doubling or seventh. Where a voicing must break something (a melody note too low to leave room under it, a note the
  figure adds that cannot be placed), the search crosses voices or leaves the added note out, and says so through the
  judge's *crossing* and *missing*.
- **Bach's own row is not a like-for-like calibration.** It reads his voices on the beat only: his suspensions,
  appoggiaturas and passing notes on the beat count as *foreign* or *missing*, and two on-beat chords with notes moving
  between them can count as parallels. The search writes chord tones only; the incidental notes are step 5.
- Not yet: diminution (step 5); variety in the inner voices (the search can draw them as well as take the cheapest;
  the four-part settings here take the cheapest).
