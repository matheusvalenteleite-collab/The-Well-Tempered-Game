# The chorale generator, step 5: diminution

Generated by `node tools/chorales/diminution_report.ts`. Code: `src/chorale/generate/diminution.ts`; the kinds of
embellishment, what a note can take and how it is written are the game's own (`src/chorale/level6.ts`); where Bach puts
them is learnt from his 370 chorales reduced by `level6.ts reduce` (each tune held out below).

At every chord the alto's, tenor's and bass's choices are made together: each weighted by how often Bach makes it in
that context (the voice, the move to the next note, the place in the phrase, the note's length), and the surface they
make costed by its rarity in Bach (parallel fifths/octaves made by an incidental note, two incidental notes a second or
seventh apart, an augmented second).

## 1. Bach's chorales, reduced, each tune held out (367 chorales; 3 not read)

Bach's chorales are read one chord per on-beat melody attack, as the generator writes, and his embellishments placed on
those chords. *Embellished*: the share of the notes that can take an embellishment that take one.

On his own reduced voices (each voice in its own rhythm), Bach embellishes 15% of the alto's notes that can take an
embellishment, 15% of the tenor's, 16% of the bass's. Read one chord per beat, as below, some of his are lost (an
embellishment after a note held over two beats, for instance), so his row below understates him a little.

Drawn as learnt, the chain embellishes more than Bach, and its surplus figures repeat generically through the chorale
(docs/chorales/MOTIVES.md); a restraint on the passing note, where its surplus lies (2^-r as likely: alto 1.5, tenor 1.6, bass 3.8 bits),
calibrated on the tunes held out, brings it to Bach's density with his balance of kinds; it is the default.

## Reading

- **The most likely choice is the same choice every time.** Where Bach usually embellishes (a third in the bass, filled
  by a passing note more often than not), the most likely setting always does; where he sometimes does, it never does.
  It embellishes about as much as the drawn settings, but without their variety: blandness here is sameness, not
  plainness. The drawn settings vary as Bach does, note by note.
- **Density.** Drawn, the generator embellishes somewhat more than Bach in this reading, most in the bass, partly because
  the reading loses some of Bach's (above). The proportions of kind follow his by voice, with fewer suspensions in the
  alto and tenor: his suspensions depend on the rhythm of the other voices, which this context does not see.
- **The surface is cleaner than Bach's** (fewer parallel fifths/octaves made by passing notes, fewer clashes): the cost of
  a rarity keeps the generator off it, where Bach sometimes goes. A case for the owner's distinction between rare and
  bad and rare and good (see the plan for Bach's exceptions).
- **Where** it embellishes agrees with Bach only in part (the column *the same kind where Bach has one*): Bach's choice
  at a note depends on more than its context here (the text, the line's shape over several notes, the other voices'
  rhythm). A finer context is the next refinement, measured here.
- Not yet: embellishment of the soprano (the melody is given); escapes, appoggiaturas and free notes (Bach's rarer kinds,
  not among the game's level-6 kinds); repeated notes tied, as Bach often writes them.
