# A newcomer's first two hours

## What the owner is really after

- **The fugue as a reward you can only open with your own hands.** The peak moment is hearing a fugue whole for the first time *because you are playing it*. Anything that lets you hear it whole beforehand spends that moment.
- **Learning that feels like being let in on a secret.** Teaching should come from someone who loves the music, not from a course. Separate tutorials feel like homework.
- **A guide, not a menu.** Twenty-four tiles on the first screen ask a non-musician to choose before they know what any of it means.
- **Honest challenge.** A fugue should be as hard as it is. A crowd that lets everyone through makes winning mean nothing.
- **A world that grows.** New rooms, new instruments, a new Glenn each time. That is the reason to come back.
- **The fear underneath:** a friend opens the game, sits through lessons, fails a fugue and never comes back.

## Three other architectures (not judged yet)

**A. The Thinned Score (quite unlike the owner's plan).** After BASICS, every fugue is open, but at first only in its **sketch** version: you play the first note of each beat of your voice, and the game plays the rest of your line quietly. A fugue climbs three levels, SKETCH, LINE and BACH. Guitar Hero and Rock Band do the same thing with their Easy charts: the same song, fewer notes. There are no tutorial pieces. Each level of each fugue *is* the lesson, and Glenn's LEARN video sits on every tile. The crowd stays the same at every level, and only the number of notes changes.

**B. Glenn's Programme (a TV season).** There is no selection screen at first. The game is a numbered series of 15-minute episodes, each one a Glenn segment plus one playable piece (a prelude, an invention, a fugue or an instrument showcase), in an order he chooses. An episode ends with a teaser ("next time, the organ loft, which I will try not to enjoy"). After episode 8 the full book appears as "the library", and the episodes carry on beside it.

**C. The Circle of Keys (a map).** Book I becomes a map of 24 houses arranged around the circle of fifths. Each house holds that key's prelude and fugue. You start in C major. Finishing a house opens its neighbours: G major, F major, A minor. You pick your route, and instruments live in neighbourhoods (the organ quarter, the harpsichord quarter). Glenn meets you at each door.

**D. The owner's option 4.** Preludes are the tutorial pieces. Finishing one opens the next prelude and the fugue paired with it. A prelude finished with an A becomes a normal piece.

## Judged against the motivations

| | Saves the fugue's first hearing | Learning feels like a secret | No overwhelm | Honest crowd | A world that grows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Thinned Score | **Weak.** At SKETCH the game plays your line for you, so you hear the whole fugue while doing little | Medium | **Weak.** Everything is open | Good | Weak |
| B. Programme | Good | **Strong** | **Strong** | Good | Strong, but **rigid**: one hard episode blocks the series |
| C. Circle | Good | Medium | Medium. Three choices at a time is fine, but neighbouring keys are not in order of difficulty | **Weak.** Your route can lead into I/2 or I/24 early | Strong |
| D. Option 4 | **Strong** | Strong | Strong | **Strong.** Fugues are never thinned | Strong |

A spends the very thing the owner wants to protect. C sounds lovely, but difficulty doesn't follow the circle. B gets the tone right but is a corridor. Option 4 wins, with one flaw: **pairing fugue *n* with prelude *n* forces Bach's numbering onto the fugues**, and the measured order puts I/2 far down the list (the assessment: I/1, then I/22, then I/23, with I/18 later).

## Recommendation: option 4, with three changes taken from B and A

1. **The preludes follow the fugues' difficulty path, not Bach's numbers.** Each prelude is picked so that it teaches what its own fugue will ask of you.
2. **Preludes bring a new skill; fugues bring a new sound.** Each unlock adds exactly one new thing. A fugue is never where you learn a skill, but it *is* where you meet a new instrument, which costs no skill and gives a lot of pleasure.
3. **Thinning only in preludes** (from A, and already in the owner's prelude idea). Your notes come on in layers, and the crowd judges only the notes you have been taught. The rest of the prelude still scrolls past and plays.

**Protecting the first hearing.** Glenn plays at most a few bars at a time, and the box goes blank when he stops. A fugue's LEARN never plays its last page. Because the crowd can end a run, *you hear how a fugue ends only by finishing it*. The ending is the prize.

### The first 30 minutes

- **0:00–0:40** The intro: B-A-C-H on the traverso (D406). No grid. Glenn's room appears instead.
- **0:40–3:30 BASICS.** Strips, gold line, higher note higher, two thumbs, on the real screen. You play only the four notes of B-A-C-H, then a ten-note answer. That's all.
- **3:30–6:30 Prelude I/1, LEARN, chapter 1.** Glenn at the piano turns sideways to you, like Professor Oak. "This is the most overplayed piece in the repertoire. Gounod put a hymn on top of it, which tells you how little it needs one." He plays bars 1–4 and stops: one chord a bar, broken the same way every time. He jumps ahead to the low note near the end that won't move. The box stays blank in between. His words ride on the notes as flags.
- **6:30–8:00 Chapter 2, how to play it** (marked on the scrub bar): the real game screen with Glenn's face in the corner. "Your job, for now: the first bass note of each bar. One note. I could do it with my elbow." He quotes Busoni's marking and teases it while showing what it means.
- **8:00–11:00 PLAY, pass 1.** The whole prelude at a gentle tempo. You play only the 35 bass notes, and you see every other note.
- **11:00–11:30** Glenn: "Adequate. Now the top note of each bar's figure."
- **11:30–14:30 Pass 2.** Bass plus the high note on beat 3.
- **14:30–15:30** One quick repeat of the new move: four bars, then four more.
- **15:30–19:00 Pass 3.** The whole broken chord, split across both thumbs, slower than written.
- **19:00–22:00 Pass 4.** Everything you've been taught, at tempo. Grade.
- **22:00–23:00 The unlock.** Two cards slide in: **Fugue I/1** and **Prelude I/22**. Glenn: "You've earned a fugue. Do try not to ruin it."
- **23:00–25:30 Fugue I/1, LEARN.** What a fugue is (one tune that keeps coming back, 24 times here). Glenn plays the subject, then two entries piling up, and stops right as it gets good.
- **25:30–30:00 Fugue I/1, PLAY.** If you fail twice, SLOW is offered (from the assessment).

### The first six unlocks

| Finish this | It opens | New skill (prelude) / new sound (fugue) |
|---|---|---|
| Prelude I/1 tutorial | Fugue I/1 (piano) + Prelude I/22 | The fugue itself is the new thing / held notes |
| Prelude I/22 | Fugue I/22 on **organ** + Prelude I/23 | The organ loft / following one line among many |
| Prelude I/23 | Fugue I/23 on **harpsichord** + Prelude I/18 | The harpsichord / short notes |
| Prelude I/18 | Fugue I/18 with the **consort** + Prelude I/14 | Glenn conducting / joined against detached |
| Prelude I/14 | Fugue I/14 with the **choir** + Prelude I/6 | Breathing / steady quick notes, first ornaments |
| Prelude I/6 | Fugue I/6 on the **synthesizer** + the library | Glenn amused / the book in Bach's order |

The prelude choices are a guess. João should measure each prelude's notes per second before anything is fixed. Finishing a prelude's tutorial with an A also opens its **concert version**: all the notes, calibrated like a fugue. Fugues open instruments; preludes open pieces.

### How instruments are taught

When an instrument first appears on a fugue, its **room video** plays first: 60–90 seconds of Glenn in the new setting, on an Invention or Sinfonia excerpt (the owner's idea). He ends with one 20-second try of what this instrument changes. An organ note lasts exactly as long as you hold it. On a harpsichord an accent is a lean in time, not a harder press (D387). The video is shared by every piece with that sound and can be watched again from the room. Watch D208: these are *defaults with a reason Glenn gives*, and they can be changed after an A, as today.

### What a returning player sees

Glenn's room, never the grid. One big card holds his suggestion for what to do next, with a line about last time: "You left the C major fugue at bar 19. The crowd has forgiven you. I have not." (Hades does this: characters remark on your last attempt.) Below it sits a shelf of your pieces with their grades, and the locked pieces appear as a count ("17 more in this book"), not as tiles. Every LEARN video can be watched again. An honest escape hatch lives in Settings, **OPEN THE WHOLE BOOK**, for the musician friends who want "the C minor one" now.

## Three risky assumptions, and a 20-minute playtest for each

1. **Non-musicians will watch Glenn and not skip him.** Hand five friends the I/1 LEARN video and log their scrubbing. Afterwards ask: "What happens near the end of the piece?" It passes if at least 3 of the 5 watch it through and can name one moment.
2. **Preludes can be fun in this game.** D263 dropped them because they "weren't working out". Run passes 1–4 of Prelude I/1 (about 14 minutes). Watch for the first sign of boredom, then ask: "Another pass, or the fugue?" If most say "skip", cut a pass, or move the layers into a single run.
3. **Finishing the prelude means you can survive the fugue.** In the last 6 minutes, put each tester straight onto Fugue I/1 and count how many finish it in two tries, without SLOW. If fewer than half do, the prelude teaches the wrong thing, or I/1 is the wrong first fugue (try I/22).
