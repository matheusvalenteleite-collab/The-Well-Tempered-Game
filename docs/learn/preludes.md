# Preludes as teaching pieces: ways to play them

*Research note, 2026-11-01. The figures come from the game's own note data (`data/touch/pieces.json`, Book I preludes `wtc1p01`–`wtc1p24`) at the game's tempi (`src/touch/tempi.ts`), and from the regularity count in `docs/wtc/PRELUDES.md`. Animated, audible examples are in [prelude-styles.html](prelude-styles.html) (open it in any browser; it works offline).*

## The problem in one paragraph

A fugue is a conversation of separate voices, so "play one voice" is a natural game. Most preludes are not built that way. Some are a single chord a bar, broken into a repeated pattern (Prelude 1 in C). Some are a machine running in both hands faster than a thumb can follow (Prelude 2 runs at about six notes a second in each hand). Some are a song with an accompaniment; some are small two-voice inventions. Earlier attempts asked the player to tap a hand's notes. This failed in Prelude 1: it was "finger acrobatics" (D259, D261). It worked better once the right thumb took only the top of each figure and the left thumb held the bass (D261). So each prelude needs a way of playing that suits its texture. Those ways can also teach, one at a time, the skills the fugues need.

## 2. Six ways to play

In every style the screen stays the phone's LADDER: two thumb strips at the edges, higher notes higher, the whole music always visible. The player's notes are drawn bold; the game's notes are drawn thin and faint.

**A. Outer voices.** The left thumb plays the bass, the right thumb the top note. Notes with tails are held; notes without are tapped. *Fits* 1, 5, 6, 12, 24. *Teaches* two independent thumbs, tap against hold, and judging pitch by height: the core of LADDER. *Fun:* calm, and musical at once. *Knobs:* how many bass or top notes per bar; tails or not. *Phone:* two coloured lines, warm on the left and cool on the right. This is D261's design, which the owner already liked. The other styles build on it.

**B. Catch the layers.** The music starts nearly empty. Play a layer (the bass, the top, an inner part) cleanly for a bar or two and it is "caught": it then plays itself while you go after the next one. A caught layer slowly fades and must be caught again. *Fits* the machines (2, 6, 1, the run in 10). *Teaches* you to hear a texture as separate strands, which is the fugue listener's basic skill. *Fun:* you build the piece yourself, and the sound gets fuller as you succeed. *Knobs:* how long a layer stays caught; how many layers. *Precedent:* Harmonix's *Amplitude* and *Frequency*, where a track played cleanly for a phrase goes on by itself while you move to another.

**C. Slow strands.** A chord a bar is really five slow tunes, one note a bar each. The player picks one strand and holds its note through the bar, heard as a sung tone above the figure. Where the note stays, keep holding. Where it moves, lift your thumb and touch the new height. *Fits* 1 above all, then 12 and 22. *Teaches* how voices move (stay, step, leap). That is exactly LADDER's up / down / same reading, and it is what a fugue's voices are. *Fun:* meditative; the big moment is finding a tune you did not know was there. In bars 12–15 of Prelude 1 the tenor slips down by half steps: B♭, A, A♭, G. *Knobs:* which strand; switching strands mid-piece.

**D. Follow the motif.** A short shape travels from hand to hand. The player plays only its arrivals, the first note or two of each statement, on whichever strip the shape is in. Missing a hand-over costs more than missing a note. *Fits* the inventions and toccatas (3, 7, 11, 13–21, 23). *Teaches* the most important fugue skill: the theme moves between voices, so keep your ear on it. *Fun:* watching for the hand-over, and the satisfaction of catching it. *Knobs:* how many notes of each statement to play; whether hand-overs are announced in advance.

**E. Surf the line.** For runs too fast to tap, the game plays every note. The player keeps one thumb down and slides it along the strip, following the line's rise and fall like a surfer on a wave. Points are earned for staying close and for landing on the turning points. *Fits* 2's ending, 3, 5, 10's fast finish, 14, 15, 21. *Teaches* the shape of fast lines, and LADDER's slide (a held thumb slid along plays the next note). *Fun:* flow, speed without panic. *Knobs:* how close counts; turning points only, or every peak. This needs an exception to D271 (no sliding back), since a surfing thumb goes both ways. Loosely like *Audiosurf*'s ride along a song.

**F. The singer.** For the song preludes the player plays the melody, but the scoring rewards shaping it: joining notes smoothly, holding long notes to their full length, and swelling and fading with the thumb's position. The game plays the accompaniment and follows your loudness. *Fits* 4, 8, 9, 10 (opening), 16, 22, 23, 24. *Teaches* legato and phrasing, which the fugues' articulation scoring already counts (D218). *Fun:* expressive rather than athletic. *Knobs:* how strict the legato is; whether ornaments are played (D278).

## 3. The reveal: Prelude 1 bar by bar

Prelude 1 is the newcomer's first piece. All 35 bars are visible from the start. What the player touches grows, and the game plays the rest. One note on the data: the top note of the figure first sounds on beats 2 and 4. On beat 3 the bass is struck again, so the top's touches go on 2 and 4.

| Bars | What the player does | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Left thumb: the bass on beat 1, held two beats (C, C, B, C). One touch a bar. | The gold line, the beat, holding. Bar 3's B is the first "lower". |
| 5–8 | Plus the right thumb: the top of the figure on beat 2 (A, D, G, C). | Two thumbs, two heights. |
| 9–11 | Bass on beats 1 and 3, top on 2 and 4: one touch a beat, thumbs in turn. Bar 10 is D–F♯–C; bar 11 arrives on G major. | Alternation; a first arrival. |
| 12–19 | The left thumb moves to the tenor, holding one note a bar: B♭, A, A♭, G, F, F (hold on), D, E. The top stays on 2 and 4. Bar 19 arrives home on C ("to the cadence in C major"). | An inner voice; stay, step or move (slow strands). |
| 20–23 | Bass on 1 and 3, while the right thumb slides up the figure's three rising notes at the start of each half bar. The game plays the repeat. The bass climbs F, F♯, A♭. | The slide, at about 3.7 notes a second within the slide. |
| 24–31 | The G pedal: the left thumb holds G for eight bars (D262's "holding builds the music"). The right thumb takes the top on 2 and 4, a line climbing D, E, F, F, F♯, G (bar 29), then F, F. It slides again in bars 28–29. | Patience and tension; hearing the line climb. |
| 32–35 | The left thumb lifts and takes the low C pedal. In bars 33–34 the figure breaks into a free line, and the right thumb surfs it. Bar 35: the final chord, both thumbs. | Arrival; surfing; the ending. |

No thumb ever touches more than about one note a second, except inside a slide. The busiest stretch, bars 20–23, asks for about one gesture a second in all (four a bar), the notes of a slide about a quarter of a second apart.

## 4. Recommendation for the first three preludes

Taking the first three in the book's order:

- **Prelude 1: the reveal above.** It is built from outer voices, then slow strands, then a taste of surfing. It is the most regular piece in the book (the same figure in 94% of bars), so a newcomer can attend to one new idea at a time.
- **Prelude 2: catch the layers, then surf.** Both hands run at about six notes a second for 24 bars, too fast to play and too good to skip. Catching the bass and the top while the machine runs teaches listening. When the machine breaks (bar 25 onwards), the player surfs the free ending.
- **Prelude 3: follow the motif.** Its figure changes hands every eight bars at the start (24 against 8 notes per four bars, then the reverse). The player plays one arrival per bar on the strip where the figure is. This is the hand-over skill the fugue that follows (and every fugue) depends on.

These three teach, in order, the thumbs, hearing layers, and following a theme. That is a fair curriculum for the fugues. If the order can be free, Prelude 13, with its plain call and answer, would be a gentler first lesson for "follow" than Prelude 3, which runs at ♩=136.

*Honest limits:* the textures are read from note counts and shapes, not from a score analysis. The preludes' cadences are not in the data yet (only their section names), so the bar-11 and bar-19 arrivals come from the section names and the notes themselves.
