## 4. Against the first prototype (lab/canon/canon-at-the-octave.html)

Ten canons at the octave written by the prototype on one subject (G major, 8 bars, lag 2 bars), measured the same way (bars 3-8, where both voices sound): 509 verticals, 62% consonant, 73% on the beat; consonances 6: 102, 3: 90, 5: 67, 8: 59; one voice moves alone at 57% of the attacks; dissonances: passing 59, neighbour 44, passing (on the beat) 36, suspension (resolves down) 30, neighbour (on the beat) 18, held, other 3. Leader's melody: steps 85%, thirds 11%, fourths/fifths 4%, sixths+ 0%; units in sequence 5 of 60.

**What this shows.** On these local measures the prototype is within Bach's range (consonance, imperfect consonances first, complementary rhythm). Two local differences: its line is more stepwise and never leaps a sixth or more, and 28% of its dissonances are passing or neighbour notes on the beat, against 4-16% in Bach's canons (25% in Var. 6, the canon at the second). Neither explains why it sounds aimless. What it lacks must be measured at a higher level: harmonic plan and cadences, phrase, motive and its sequential working, register and climax, and (in the Goldberg canons) the bass. That is the next study (PLAN.md, K3-K5).
