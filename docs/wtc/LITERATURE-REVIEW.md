# Bach's fugues: verified literature review

For the FUGUE study: the 48 WTC fugues first, then the Art of Fugue (AoF), the two ricercars of the Musical Offering (MO), and the fugue of BWV 903. Compiled 12 October 2026.

**Rule followed.** Every row below was checked against at least one bibliographic source, named in the last column. These were Crossref (publisher DOI metadata), publisher, OUP or Cambridge pages, library catalogues, Internet Archive, the journal page, or the author's own list. Where a detail could not be confirmed, the row says **unverified**. The "what it establishes" column reports only what an abstract, table of contents, review or the text itself confirms. My own inferences are marked *(inference)*.

**Bach-Jahrbuch years.** Crossref dates the Bach-Jahrbuch (BJ) DOIs to their 2018 digitisation. The real year is encoded in the DOI (`bjb.v1927…` = 1927) and is the one given here. Every BJ volume is open on qucosa (bjb.journals.qucosa.de).

**Tiers.** 1 = read now. 2 = consult for a named question. 3 = not relevant (reason given).

## Datasets and annotations

1. **Algomus fugue reference dataset (Lille).** This is the only openly licensed, machine-readable gold standard for Bach fugues that I found.
   - **Where.** https://gitlab.com/algomus.fr/algomus-data/tree/master/fugues. The single file is `fugues/fugues.ref`, 1,093 lines, and there are per-piece `.dez` files in `fugues/bach-wtc-i/`. Browsable on Dezrann at http://www.dezrann.net/explore/bach-fugues; that link is listed in the README, but I did not open it.
   - **Coverage.** The **24 fugues of WTC I only** (BWV 846–869; the README's "BWV 846-893" is a typo), plus 12 Shostakovich fugues. **Nothing for WTC II, the AoF, the MO or BWV 903.**
   - **Labels.** Subject (S) occurrences per voice, including tonal answers (flagged `tonal_answer`) and augmented subjects (Saug). Incomplete subject heads (S-inc, partly). Countersubjects CS1 and CS2. Cadences (PAC, root-position IAC, strong HC/DC, with key, e.g. `17 (v:PAC)`). Pedals. Offsets are in bars from the first complete bar, with fine offsets for upbeats. Example: BWV 846 has S in S 2.5, 7, 16.25, 20.75…, and cadences at 14 (vi:PAC), 19.5 (II:PAC), 24 (I:PAC).
   - **Sources behind the annotations.** Prout 1910, Tovey 1924, Keller 1965, Bruhn 1993/2006, Charlier 2009, earsense.org, and the team's own analysis. About 610 labels in the first Bach release. In 8 of the 24 fugues at least two sources disagree on where the subject ends; the file gives the alternatives. Scores came from KernScores `.krn`.
   - **Licence and citation.** ODbL 1.0, with contents under DbCL 1.0. Cite Giraud et al., CMJ 2015 (#45).
   - **Caveat.** The `.dez` folder has no `11-bwv856-ref.dez`, though `fugues.ref` covers all 24 (wtc-i-11 at line 348). Some `-computed.dez` files hold algorithm output, not ground truth.
   - **Use for the project** *(inference)*. An external check of the hand-read "gold standard" on WTC I subjects, countersubjects and cadences. It needs a bar-offset conversion.
2. **Symbolic scores.** KernScores (kern.humdrum.org) hosts Humdrum encodings of the WTC fugues, both books. CCARH credits the encodings to David Huron. This comes from the CCARH Humdrum page via search; the site returned 503 here, so the file list and licence are **unverified**. Whether an AoF or MO encoding exists there is **unverified**. GitHub was blocked by the session proxy, so I could not check the humdrum-data repositories.
3. **No open dataset found** for: WTC II, AoF or MO entry annotations; stretto lists (McDonald 2019 documents every stretto in the 48, but the data sit only in the thesis PDF); episode labels beyond the Algomus WTC I harmonic-sequence work; dux–comes voice order (Eichert 2002 has a printed list only).
4. **Printed per-fugue inventories that could be keyed in as second opinions** *(inference)*: Groocock 2003 (all 48: structure, episodes, stretto, countersubjects); Dürr 1998 (a diagram per fugue); Prout 1910 (all 48, open on Internet Archive); Eichert 2002 (dux–comes dispositions).
