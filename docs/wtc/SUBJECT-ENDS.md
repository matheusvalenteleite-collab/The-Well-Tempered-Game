# Where the subject ends: the settled subjects against the literature (for the owner)

12 October 2026. The settled subjects (`SUBJECTS.md`) were read under a protocol that let a subject run until the
next entry took over. Groocock (2003) ends a subject where its melodic sense closes and calls what follows a codetta;
the Algomus reference (from Prout, Tovey, Keller, Bruhn, Charlier) and the later statements in the fugues themselves
(what Bach keeps when he restates the subject: `ALGOMUS.md`, I/11) mostly point the same way. Tomita's notes to
Groocock (`data/wtc/groocock/notes.json`) give the spread of readings; the cases where ours differs:

| fugue | ours | Groocock | others (Tomita's notes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I/7 | to 3:1 | 4th note of bar 2 | (no note on the end) |
| I/11 | to 5:1 (through the sixteenths) | 1st note of bar 4, then a codetta | Riemann, Busoni: 3rd note of bar 4; Czaczkes, Bruhn, Dürr: countersubject from the 2nd note of bar 4; Algomus 3 bars; Keller as Groocock |
| II/7 | to 7:1 | bar 6, disputed by himself | most (Dehn, Higgs, Iliffe, Sampson, Knorr, Macpherson, Bruhn, Dürr): 1st note of bar 7, as ours; Jadassohn, Riemann, Stade, Czaczkes: bar 6 |
| II/15 | to 8:1 | 1st note of bar 6 (Tomita: the most convincing) | Riemann, Souchay: 3rd note of bar 6; Dürr bar 7; Keller bar 8, as ours |
| II/23 | to 6:1 | 1st note of bar 4 | (no note on the end) |
| II/20 | to 3:3 | 1st note of bar 3 | Keller: 5th note of bar 3, as ours |
| II/3 | 1½ bars (12 notes) | 1½ bars, but most entries keep only the head | 12 notes: Tovey, David, Kennan, Schulenberg; 6 notes: Riemann, Stade, Sampson, Macpherson, Knorr, Czaczkes, Bruhn, Dürr; 4: Iliffe, Brandts Buys; 8: Keller |

Not a single right answer: II/7 shows ours with the majority, II/15 ours with Keller alone. The question for the
owner is the criterion (what later statements keep; where the melodic sense closes; until the next entry), then the
cases. II/3 is a different matter: the analysis must find the statements of the head, whatever the subject's length.
