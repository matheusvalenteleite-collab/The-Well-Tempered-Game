## What stands out

A first reading of the counts below; each point is to be checked in the pieces named.

1. **The head rule holds without exception.** Every subject that begins on ^5 (20 of the 61) is
   answered from ^1: the tonic answers the dominant at the head, in the 48 as in the late works.
   It is the one candidate for a boundary that the corpus offers without a counter-example.
2. **The tonal answer is a matter of the head.** 35 answers are tonal; in 30 of them the first
   mutated note is the first or second. Beyond the head the answer is a transposition.
3. **Subjects begin on ^1 or ^5** (58 of 61; II/13 on ^7, II/21 on ^2, the new subject of AoF X
   on the raised seventh) **and end on ^1, ^3 or ^5** (57 of 61). A subject states its key.
4. **Links come before the third entry, not before the answer.** A codetta between subject and
   answer in 8 of 61; before the third entry in 37 of 60. The answer follows at once; the third
   entry is prepared.
5. **The late works differ from the 48 in two habits.** Their subjects begin on the downbeat (all
   but the three new subjects of AoF VIII-X, and both ricercars), where the 48's mostly begin
   after it (34 of 48); and their answers are tonal (AoF I-IV, both ricercars, BWV 903), where the
   48 are divided (28 tonal, 20 real). The stile antico's long notes and the old answer within
   the mode, on one side; the keyboard fugue's upbeat subjects, on the other.

Fux prescribes the exchange of fifth and fourth that keeps the answer within the mode's octave (a leap ^1-^5 answered by ^5-^1, *Gradus* 1725, p. 144), not a mapping of degrees; and he prefers, where the two conflict, keeping the subject's intervals ("potior est enim ratio Cantus, quam Modi", p. 232; docs/fux/fugue.md). Bach's practice is the degree rule: in the 20 subjects beginning on ^5 the first note is answered by ^1, whether it is left by a leap or by a step. ## Limits of part 2 (to be removed as R2 is done)

Parts 4-7 count what the study's analysis finds. Where it is blind, the counts are low, and the late works
suffer most:

- **Strettos are counted between ordinary entries only.** Entries in augmentation or diminution are found
  apart and not paired: AoF VI (a counter-fugue in diminution, stretto throughout) shows none, AoF VII
  too few. Inverted entries are paired.
- **Augmentation and diminution are found only where the rhythm is the subject's scaled exactly:** AoF
  VI's dotted French diminution is missed.
- **Later subjects are known only for the 48** (data/wtc/later-subjects.json, from Ledbetter): the second
  and third subjects of AoF VIII-X and their combinations are not yet counted.
- **Varied entries** (a changed head, an ornamented statement, the Thema Regium decorated in the Ricercar
  a 3) are missed; the Musical Offering's and BWV 903's entry densities are lower bounds, and their
  episode shares upper bounds.
- **An episode here is any bar or more without a found entry,** codettas included; the structure study's
  44% (docs/wtc/structure-study.md) used another boundary. The comparison between sets holds; the level
  is a convention.
- **The degrees of later entries are transpositions from the first entry,** not keys: an answer counts
  as V, an entry in the relative minor of a major fugue as VI. They agree with the key-plan study (I 30%,
  V 27%, vi 14%, IV 10% in major) where they overlap.
