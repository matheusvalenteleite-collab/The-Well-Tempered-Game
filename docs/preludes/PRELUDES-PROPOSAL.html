Where the preludes go

Three ways to bring the preludes back to the phone, and a recommendation. Nothing is built yet.

What went wrong last time

The preludes left the phone (D263) for two reasons. They were a second game, on a different screen (HIGHWAY) from the fugues' LADDER. And each one needed its own rules: Prelude 1 took thirteen rounds of design, and went from "finger acrobatics" to "much better" only when each thumb touched about one note a second (D261).

So a prelude works when it is the same game as the fugue and asks little of the thumbs. All three concepts are judged against that.

The progression session is already working on your own "option 4" (preludes as the tutorial pieces). That is Concept A below. I judge it here and do not duplicate its work.

Three concepts

Concept A · the prelude teaches its fugue

The Door

The player
Before each new fugue, plays a prelude in passes, one layer more each time (bass, then top, then more). Each pass brings one skill. Finishing it opens the fugue.
Why fun
Every prelude has a purpose, and the fugue is the prize. Learning happens inside the music, with no separate tutorial.
Cost
High. Each prelude used needs its own layer plan and Glenn's script. Across sixteen stages it would be the biggest content job in the game.
Main risk
It puts a prelude in front of the thing players came for. If the prelude is dull, they stop at the door. The progression note lists this as its own riskiest assumption. Only eight preludes carry a tune that maps straight onto a fugue skill.

Concept B · a different kind of play

The Interlude

The player
Between fugues, plays a short piece with its own rules. Catch the layers: a part played cleanly goes on by itself while you catch the next. Surf a fast run by sliding. Hold a slow tune hidden inside the chords. There is no crowd to lose, only a score.
Why fun
A change of pace, as Bach's pairs are: free, then strict. The machines (I/2, I/3, I/15) become the most exciting pieces instead of the hardest.
Cost
High, and new. Two or three new mechanics. Surfing needs the thumb to slide both ways, which D271 rules out. Each prelude needs its rules chosen.
Main risk
A second game to learn, which is exactly what D263 removed. It takes attention away from LADDER.

Concept C · same game, optional · recommended

The Overture

The player
On a fugue's card, beside ▶ PLAY, an optional ▶ PRELUDE FIRST. One line of the prelude on LADDER, chosen by Glenn: the tune, the walking bass, or the top notes. It is thinned so it is never harder than its fugue. It runs straight on into the fugue, in the same key and sound. Finish the prelude and the fugue's crowd starts warm: it begins fuller, so a stumble early in the fugue is forgiven.
Why fun
More music on the mechanic players already like. Two minutes to warm the thumbs before the hard piece. A real advantage for doing it, but no lock. And 24 pairs to complete, as Bach wrote them.
Cost
Low. LADDER already plays a prelude's line in the Lab, and the thinning by tier exists. The work is Glenn choosing a line for each prelude (a top line drawn out for the eight without one), the button, the crowd's head start, and running on into the fugue.
Main risk
Anything optional can be ignored, so the preludes might end up as decoration. The beginners' tier is also the weakest for it: only I/4 has a ready tune, and the others there need a top line drawn out.

Recommendation: C now, as the test for A

A depends on one unproven claim: that preludes are fun on the phone. C tests that claim cheaply, without blocking a single fugue. If your friends press PRELUDE FIRST when nothing makes them, the preludes have earned a teaching role, and the best of them can become A's stages. If they skip it, A would have forced them through something they do not want, and we will have learned that for the price of one button.

B should wait. It is the right home for the eight machines, but later, as a special event, once LADDER is settled.

For you to choose

  • A, B, C, or C then A (my choice).
  • If C: the reward. The crowd starts warm (my choice), or a mark on the pair and nothing more.
  • If C: all 24 at once with drawn-out top lines, or the eight ready tunes first (I/4, 9, 10, 13, 17, 19, 20, 24).
